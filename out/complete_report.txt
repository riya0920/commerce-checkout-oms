A. PER-LINE TAX -- TWO OF MY OWN PROJECTS DISAGREED ABOUT A NUMBER
==============================================================================
The previous README said it plainly: 'SE-2 now models per-line rates;
SE-1 has not adopted them, so the two projects disagree about tax and
SE-1 is the wrong one.' Two services in one portfolio computed a
different tax on the same cart.

  line              gross   discount    taxable  rate bp        tax
  TEE-BLUE         $49.98     -$8.00     $41.98      875      $3.67
  MILK             $13.47     -$2.16     $11.31        0      $0.00
  HEADPHONES       $89.99    -$14.40     $75.59      875      $6.61
  TOTAL                                                      $10.28

A single cart rate would have charged $11.28 -- $1.00 more, by taxing the
exempt groceries.

  THERE IS NO CORRECT SINGLE CART RATE when the lines differ, which is
  exactly why the order-level discount has to be ALLOCATED to lines
  before tax is computable at all. That is the same allocation SE-2's
  promo engine and this project's partial refunds both stand on: get it
  wrong and you are wrong twice, in two systems, months apart.

  MANUFACTURER vs RETAILER discount on the same line:
    retailer-funded     taxable $41.98  tax $3.67
    manufacturer-funded taxable $49.98  tax $4.37
  A retailer discount reduces the taxable receipt; a manufacturer
  coupon does not, because the retailer is reimbursed. $0.70 of tax on one
  line, and it is a flag rather than prose because somebody will ask.

B. POLICIES THAT WERE CONSTANTS
==============================================================================
Repricing between cart and checkout, per CATEGORY and per TIER:

  category     tier         quoted   current     decision    charged
  apparel      standard     $50.00    $52.00     honoured     $50.00
  apparel      gold         $50.00    $52.00     honoured     $50.00
  electronics  standard   $1200.00  $1220.00    reconfirm          -
  electronics  gold       $1200.00  $1220.00     honoured   $1200.00
  grocery      standard      $4.49     $4.70     honoured      $4.49
  apparel      standard     $50.00    $45.00      reduced     $45.00

  The same +$20 rise is HONOURED for a standard apparel shopper and
  RECONFIRMED for a standard electronics one, because the electronics
  rule caps at 2% and $20 on a $1,200 item is under the cash cap but
  over the percentage. The tolerance is the SMALLER of the two, so the
  percentage binds on expensive items and the cash cap on cheap ones.

  The tier column is a real and slightly uncomfortable decision: a
  programme that absorbs more for high-value customers IS price
  discrimination by tenure. It is legal and widespread, and it belongs
  in a table somebody signed off rather than an if-statement somebody
  wrote.

Returns: shipping, windows and restocking.

  category       days   full        merch     shipping      restock     refund
  apparel          10   True       $50.00        $7.95        $0.00     $57.95
  apparel          10  False       $50.00        $0.00        $0.00     $50.00
  apparel          90   True       $50.00        $0.00        $0.00    REFUSED
  electronics       3   True       $50.00        $7.95        $0.00     $57.95
  electronics      20   True       $50.00        $7.95       -$7.50     $50.45
  grocery           3   True       $50.00        $0.00        $0.00     $50.00

  SHIPPING COMES BACK ON A FULL RETURN AND NOT ON A PARTIAL ONE, and
  the reason is not arbitrary: a partial return still required the
  parcel, so the shipping was consumed. A full return means the
  shipment should not have happened.

  Electronics carry a restocking fee outside a 7-day grace period, so
  the fast returns that correlate with a sizing or spec mistake are
  free and the slow ones -- which correlate with use -- are not.

  The 90-day apparel row is REFUSED, and refusing is a policy outcome
  the API has to be able to express. The previous version had no window
  at all, which is not a generous policy, it is an absent one.

Exchanges, including the case the previous pass got wrong:

  even swap                  $32.63 -> $32.63 : none          even exchange moves no money at all
  upgrade                    $32.63 -> $39.15 : capture       customer pays the difference
  cheaper, above the floor   $39.15 -> $32.63 : refund        customer is refunded the difference
  cheaper, below the floor   $32.63 -> $32.00 : store_credit  below the refund floor: issued as credit

  A CHEAPER REPLACEMENT IS NOT SIMPLY A NET REFUND. Below a floor the
  refund costs more to process than it returns -- payment fees, a
  statement line the customer queries, a support contact -- so small
  differences become store credit. The floor is a merchant input, and
  stating it is the point: the alternative is a silent rounding that
  customers notice and support cannot explain.

C. ALLOCATION THAT CAN SEE TOMORROW
==============================================================================
Near DC: 12 units, 3.0 days of cover. Alternative DC is deep in stock
and further away. Where does the far DC start to win?

  far DC km   near ship     far ship near penalty     near total       winner
  100             $7.06       $10.50        $5.40         $12.46          FAR
  300             $7.06       $18.50        $5.40         $12.46         near
  600             $7.06       $30.50        $5.40         $12.46         near
  900             $7.06       $42.50        $5.40         $12.46         near
  1500            $7.06       $66.50        $5.40         $12.46         near
  3585            $7.06      $149.90        $5.40         $12.46         near

  A MYOPIC SCORER PICKS THE NEAR DC ON EVERY ROW (confirmed), because shipping
  from 14 km is cheaper than shipping from anywhere. The forward-aware
  scorer adds $5.40 for draining a DC that has 3.0 days of cover, and
  that flips the decision once the far DC is within 100 km.

  The flip point is the useful output, not the winner. It says how
  much scarcity is worth in shipping-distance terms, which is a
  number a network planner can argue with -- and it is entirely a
  function of two merchant constants ($6.50 per parcel, $0.04 per km).

  HONEST LIMIT: the penalty is a cover-shortfall heuristic, not a
  forward-position model. The real version needs a demand forecast per
  DC, which is ML-1's job and is not wired in here.

D. A LOAD TEST THAT IS ACTUALLY A LOAD TEST
==============================================================================
The previous one was a thread pool: no think time, no separate client
process, so p99 contained Python's own scheduler. Three distortions,
all in the same direction.

  closed loop (think 40ms)   throughput    59.1/s   ok 1.000
                             service   p50   4.33  p95 119.90  p99 213.77 ms
                             perceived p50   4.33  p95 119.90  p99 213.77 ms

  open loop (arrivals 6ms)   throughput   729.9/s   ok 1.000
                             service   p50   0.09  p95   0.24  p99   0.94 ms
                             perceived p50   1.22  p95  44.57  p99  64.13 ms

  COORDINATED OMISSION, MEASURED.

  closed loop : perceived p99 213.77 ms, service p99 213.77 ms, gap 0.00
  open loop   : perceived p99 64.13 ms, service p99 0.94 ms, gap 63.19

  The closed loop's gap is ZERO BY CONSTRUCTION, and that is the whole
  problem with it rather than a good property: a request is 'due' when
  the client becomes free, so a client that is blocked on a slow
  response is by definition not late for anything. When the system
  stalls, the closed loop STOPS SENDING -- the requests that would have
  queued behind the stall are never issued, and their latency never
  appears anywhere.

  The open loop fixes its schedule in advance, so a stall shows up as
  requests that were due and had to wait. That difference is real
  latency a user experienced and no server-side APM would ever record.

  Note the throughput column as well: 59.1/s closed against 729.9/s open.
  A closed-loop benchmark reports whatever throughput the system can
  sustain and calls it capacity, which is circular -- it measured the
  rate it chose to send.

  Separate PROCESSES matter for a duller reason: threads share the GIL
  with the server, so every microsecond a client spends parsing is a
  microsecond the server cannot run. The old number contained the
  client's own cost.

  STILL NOT A SERVICE LATENCY. Client and server are one machine with
  no network and no serialisation. These are a floor, and quoting them
  as production latency would be the same mistake in a new costume.
